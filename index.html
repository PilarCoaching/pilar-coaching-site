About Maripili

A steady presence for women in the middle of change.

I know what it feels like to rebuild: not once, but many times. To move across cultures, to hold a family together through change, to ask hard questions about faith and identity, and to keep standing.

My own path has taken me through relocation, motherhood, and the kind of transitions, turns, and challenges life quietly hands us. Along the way, I found that the moments which felt most disorienting were also the ones that taught me the most about what it means to stand firm, and to begin again.

Through every one of those seasons, my faith has been my source of strength: the steady ground beneath the changing landscape, and the reason I trust that transition can lead somewhere good.

That's the work I now do with other women. Not as someone who has it all figured out, but as someone who has walked through real transition and knows how to walk alongside you through yours.

PILAR means 'pillar' in Spanish, and it's also my own middle name. Both meanings capture what I hope to offer: steady support to stand on, drawn from my own story.

— Maripili

The PILAR Approach

Three phases. One steady path forward.

Stand
firm
Find your ground

Before anything moves forward, we slow down. We name what's actually happening, steady your footing, and reconnect you to what's true about your circumstances, your faith, and yourself.

Start
over
(Re)Build with intention

This is the active work: reshaping identity, relationships, career, or faith with clarity instead of reacting to circumstance. Change becomes something you're choosing, not just surviving.

Live
fully
Step into who you're becoming

The goal was never just to cope. It's to live with purpose, freedom, and faith on the other side of transition, fully present in the life you're building.

Who This Is For

For women navigating life's transitions, challenges & turns.

Life asks us to become someone new more often than we expect. A move, a baby, a loss, a new chapter: each one quietly shifts who you are. You may not have a name for it yet, and you do not need one. If any of this feels familiar, we should talk.
